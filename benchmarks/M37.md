# M37 global clustered index

## Offline clustering probe

Stage 2 of [the M37 design](../docs/CLUSTERED_INDEX.md): deterministic
bounded centroid training and an offline layout and query simulation. No
engine format, write path or object is involved; blocks and packs are
computed in memory.

```
cargo build --release --example m37_cluster_probe
m37_cluster_probe sift_base.fvecs sift_query.fvecs 250000 256,512,1024,2048 42
m37_cluster_probe sift_base.fvecs sift_query.fvecs 1000000 256,512,1024,2048 42
m37_cluster_probe sift_base.fvecs sift_query.fvecs 1000000 256,512,1024 42 65536 2
m37_cluster_probe sift_base.fvecs sift_query.fvecs 1000000 256,512,1024 42 16384 8
```

Raw results: [250,000 rows](m37/probe-sift1m-250000.json),
[1,000,000 rows](m37/probe-sift1m-1000000.json), and the 1,000,000-row
[65,536-row sample](m37/probe-sift1m-1000000-sample65536.json) and
[eight-iteration](m37/probe-sift1m-1000000-iterations8.json) comparisons.

**Dataset.** The M31 corpus: the TEXMEX SIFT1M archive (SHA-256
`92f1270c5e3a0cb46b89983e72b0511e4df065c31a9fa0276d8c9b1fca5bc81a`), 128
dimensions, squared Euclidean, k=10, the first 200 rows of `sift_query.fvecs`
(those rows hash to `5fa992cd…`; the whole file is the M21 digest). The
250,000-row prefix SHA-256 is `fab6b3f6…` (the M21 prefix) and the
1,000,000-row prefix is the whole `sift_base.fvecs` (`21f66e29…`). The exact
f64 top-10 with ID ties is computed in the probe; its ID lists are identical
to the recorded M24 250,000-row and M31 1,000,000-row oracles
(`exact_top10_sha256` `efb18e5c…` and `3f0c752d…`). Recall is the fraction of
the exact top-10 returned; the fifth percentile is the tenth lowest of 200
queries.

**Training.** One streaming pass keeps the 16,384 rows with the smallest
`(ivf::sample_priority(seed, ID), ID)` (seed 42); the first `centroids` rows
in that order are the initial centers, refined by two Lloyd iterations over
the sample only (`ivf::train_bounded`, means for squared Euclidean). Every
row is then assigned to its nearest final center with `(score, cluster ID)`
ties. The process peak RSS after sampling and training all four counts was
14.6 MB at 250,000 rows and 14.5 MB at 1,000,000 rows (the sample itself is
8 MiB). Afterwards the probe loads the whole prefix for assignment, layout
and the oracle (peak 214 MB and 730 MB); that is probe memory, not an engine
estimate. Centroids cost `centroids x (128 x 4 + 4)` bytes.

**Boundary duplication** (a separate configuration) gives a row one secondary
copy in its second-nearest cluster when its second/first squared-distance
ratio is among the smallest 5% of rows (ties by ID): the design's budget,
with the ratio threshold calibrated to it.

**Layout.** Each cluster's posting is sorted by ID and split greedily at 170
rows or 120 KiB of raw `GLB2` bytes. Block sizes are real: each block's raw
`GLB2` layout (M31 metadata on IDs divisible by 100, 100-row request
sequences, cluster ID as the partition) is compressed with zstd level 3 plus
the 8-byte header. Packs hold at most 12 blocks and 960 KiB of raw block
bytes, as seals do. Clusters are placed in cluster-ID order (or, as a
comparison, a greedy nearest-centroid chain from cluster 0); a cluster that
does not fit the open pack starts a new one, so it is split only when it
exceeds a pack. One range request covers one pack; a later extent in a pack
already being read widens its range and is charged the gap.

**Query modes**, each with request budgets 1/2/4/8/10/12 and byte caps
256 KiB/512 KiB/1 MiB, reranking only the chosen blocks' rows exactly:

- *Whole postings*: postings in centroid order, stopping at the first one
  that does not fit. This needs no sketches.
- *Ranked blocks*: the blocks of the nearest 1/2/4/8/16 postings are ranked by
  their exact nearest-row distance to the query and chosen in that order,
  skipping blocks that do not fit, as the segmented planner chooses spans.
  The design ranks with five-bit sketch scores instead; exact block minima
  are an optimistic stand-in, so these rows bound what sketch ranking can
  reach on this layout and are not a predicted engine result.
- *Best-8-postings ceiling*: the largest fraction of the top-10 that any eight
  postings hold together, ignoring caps (the analogue of M31's best-eight-blocks
  ceiling of 0.885).

**Environment.** Apple M4 (10 cores, 16 GiB), macOS (Darwin 25.6.0,
arm64), rustc 1.98.1, release build of `26e9323`, 10 worker threads for
assignment, encoding and queries. Other builds and probes shared the machine
(load average 10 to 85), so times are indicative only. Recall, layout and
occupancy are deterministic: an earlier run of the same code produced
identical results for both main probes apart from times and RSS.

### Findings

- **The partition holds the neighbors.** At every setting the best eight
  postings hold at least 0.9945 of the true top-10. What fails is choosing
  what to read within the caps.
- **Whole-posting reads cannot meet the gate.** An encoded row costs 157 to
  160 bytes, so 1 MiB holds about 6,600 rows. At 1,000,000 rows, few
  centroids give postings larger than the caps (256 centroids: median
  532 KiB, 24 postings over 1 MiB, 2.72 extents per cluster) and many give
  small postings that exhaust eight requests. The best whole-posting result
  at 8 requests and 1 MiB is 0.741 mean and 0.3 p5 (2,048 centroids),
  against 0.863 and 0.5 at 250,000 rows. A query whose nearest posting
  exceeds the byte cap gets no rows: up to 99% short results at 256 KiB.
  Ranked-block reads were short for at most one query (0.5%, 2,048
  centroids at 1,000,000 rows).
- **Ranked blocks within 16 probed postings reach the recall gates** (mean
  >=0.90, p5 >=0.80, no short results) at 8 requests and 1 MiB for 256
  centroids at both 250,000 rows (0.980 / 0.9) and 1,000,000 rows
  (0.979 / 0.9), and for 512 centroids at 1,000,000 rows (0.962 / 0.8).
  Recall did not fall from 250,000 to 1,000,000 rows at 256 centroids. The
  design's starting 1,024 centroids at 1,000,000 rows reach 0.927 but a p5
  of 0.7, and eight probes miss the p5 gate everywhere. Because a probed
  cluster's blocks are contiguous, eight ranges carried about 40 blocks and
  nearly 1 MiB; a 512 KiB cap kept 0.970 / 0.8 (256) and 0.957 / 0.8 (512)
  at 1,000,000 rows. This is an upper bound for sketch-ranked selection:
  stage 3 must measure the five-bit codes on real packs before a count is
  fixed.
- **Boundary duplication is not justified here.** Duplicating 5% of rows
  (squared-distance ratio <=1.007) changes 16-probe recall by -0.008 to
  +0.007 while adding 5% rows and bytes.
- **Placement order matters only for whole postings.** The centroid chain
  raises whole-posting recall when postings are small (250,000 rows, 1,024
  centroids: 0.800 to 0.851) and leaves 16-probe ranked-block recall
  unchanged at every setting.
- **The bounded profile is sufficient.** Eight iterations or a 65,536-row
  sample change 16-probe recall at 1,000,000 rows by -0.004 to +0.011 while
  training takes up to 11 times longer (the larger sample is 32 MiB of
  vectors). Occupancy is skewed either way: at 1,000,000 rows and 1,024
  centroids clusters hold 1 to 4,994 rows (p50 870, p95 2,260).
- **Cost.** Training one count took 0.4 to 3.1 s single-threaded on the
  16,384-row sample. Assigning all 1,000,000 rows took 3.1 to 36.0 s for 256
  to 2,048 centroids on 10 loaded threads; whole-view conversion (stage 3)
  pays this once per epoch.

## Stage 3: conversion and clustered queries on real packs

The engine path of [stage 3](../docs/CLUSTERED_INDEX.md): the namespace
is loaded through `SegmentedDatabase` as the M31 load does (100-row
requests, M21 one-percent metadata, a seal every 32 log objects, run
consolidation), converted with `convert_clustered` (default options: about
4,000 rows per cluster, seed 42, 16,384-row sample, two iterations, 64 MiB
gather passes), and queried cold with the persisted five-bit `GLSKT003`
posting sketches, no cache and every remote range counted. Blocks are
ranked inside the probed postings and chosen in rank order within the
request and byte caps; every block in a chosen span is reranked exactly.

```

```

Raw results: [250,000 rows](m37/conversion-sift1m-250000.json),

**Dataset and oracle** as in stage 2: the exact top-10 hashes
(`efb18e5c…`, `3f0c752d…`) equal the recorded M24 and M31 oracles. The store
is in memory (a counting `ObjectStore`), so request counts and bytes are
exact but latency is not an object-store measurement; recall does not depend
on the backend. **Environment:** Apple M4 (10 cores, 16 GiB), macOS
(Darwin 25.6.0, arm64), rustc 1.98.1, release build of this branch, 8 query
threads.

### Findings

- **The gates hold on real packs.** With 16 probes, 8 range requests and
  1 MiB, mean / p5 recall@10 is 0.994 / 0.9 at 250,000 rows and 0.970 / 0.8
  at 1,000,000 rows with no short results, against 0.9445 / 0.8 and
  0.894 / 0.8 for the per-seal layout at the M31 budget measured in the same
  runs (the 1,000,000-row per-seal value reproduces M31's 0.894). Five-bit
  ranking costs about 0.01 against stage 2's exact-minimum bound at 1M rows
  (0.979 / 0.9 for 256 centroids).
- **p5 at 1,000,000 rows sits on the gate.** 32 probes give 0.981 / 0.9 within
  the same caps; 512 KiB keeps 0.958 / 0.8; 4 requests or 256 KiB fail. The
  default stays 16 probes until the update-wave measurement (stage 4).
- **Every query used at most 8 ranges and 1 MiB**, nearly all of both: about
  40 contiguous blocks per query.
- **Cost.** Conversion took 2.0 s at 250,000 rows (64 centroids, 3 gather
  passes, 172 posting packs, 173 extents) and 14.9 s at 1,000,000 rows
  (256 centroids, 10 gather passes, 689 posting packs, 697 extents, 1.01
  extents per cluster). Posting payload equals the canonical payload
  (259.1 MB vs 249.3 MB at 1M), so visible payload doubles while canonical
  runs are retained. The loaded routing state is smaller than the per-seal
  one (100.4 MB vs 115.4 MB at 1M) because only posting rows are routed;
  the per-row sequence costs 8 B. Sketches were never rebuilt.

## Stages 4-5: clustered seals and posting merges under the update wave

The same probe with a `ROUNDS` argument converts as in stage 3, then replays
the M31 update wave through `SegmentedServing` (M31 serving options without
a cache): each round, four writers overwrite 100 IDs of their quarter with
generation r+1 (base row `(id + 137 g) % rows`), published as two
group-commit logs (about the 1.9 logs/s of the M31 MinIO run), and idle
maintenance (clustered seals at 32 logs, posting merges, consolidation,
pruning, reclamation, cleanup) runs to completion. Cold recall is then
measured with 16 probes against the exact top-10 of the updated corpus
(the same 200 queries). Object creates and uploaded bytes are counted per
key kind in the in-memory store; rates divide by the 300 rounds as if each
round were one second, so they model the M31 offered load, not wall time.

```
cargo build --release --example m37_conversion_probe
m37_conversion_probe sift_base.fvecs sift_query.fvecs 250000 - 300
m37_conversion_probe sift_base.fvecs sift_query.fvecs 1000000 - 300
```

Raw results: [250,000 rows](m37/wave-sift1m-250000.json),
[1,000,000 rows](m37/wave-sift1m-1000000.json). Environment as in stage 3
(Apple M4, macOS, rustc 1.98.1), release build of the
`feature/m37-clustered-seals` engine (commit `b09b741`).

| Rows | Static, 16 probes, 8 req / 1 MiB | After wave, 8 req / 1 MiB | 8 req / 512 KiB | 12 req / 1 MiB | Short | PUT/s | Upload MiB/s | Seals / merge rounds |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 250,000 | 0.994 / 0.9 | 0.977 / 0.9 | 0.9765 / 0.9 | 0.9995 / 1.0 | 0 | 3.62 | 0.98 | 18 / 15 |
| 1,000,000 | 0.970 / 0.8 | 0.9585 / 0.8 | 0.9535 / 0.8 | 0.982 / 0.9 | 0 | 4.90 | 1.68 | 18 / 18 |

At 1,000,000 rows the creates were 600 logs, 653 packs, 109 roots
(245 MB, about 2.25 MB each), 71 indexes and 36 catalogs (13.8 MB).
The final view had 1,416 extents over 256 clusters (452 canonical, 964
derived; at most 11 per cluster, at most 3 small), 803 posting packs, no
uncovered canonical pack, 115.1 MB of charged routing state (100.4 MB
after conversion) and 582 MB of visible objects. Every query used at most 8
ranges and 1 MiB.

Findings:

- **The update-wave recall gates hold without split or reassignment**
  (stage 6 is not needed by this evidence): mean >=0.90, p5 >=0.80 and no
  short results at both scales. At 1,000,000 rows p5 stays exactly on the
  gate, as it was statically; 12 requests or 32 probes (stage 3) give
  margin but are not the declared budget.
- **Publications, not packs, dominate upload.** With a six-block small
  threshold and 16 output groups per round, the 1,000,000-row replay made
  38 merge rounds, 5.93 PUT/s and 2.13 MiB/s (over the 2 MiB/s gate); 32
  groups per round cut it to 22 rounds and 2.00 MiB/s; a three-block
  threshold (kept) to 18 rounds, 4.90 PUT/s and 1.68 MiB/s, for 0.003 less
  mean recall. Each root create carries every block reference.
- **Seal fragments are small.** About 6,400 rows per seal over 256 clusters
  is 25 rows per cluster: about 256 blocks and 22 packs per seal under the
  12-block pack limit.
- These are in-memory counts; latency, peak RSS and the remaining gates
  need the MinIO or S3 acceptance with `--clustered`.
